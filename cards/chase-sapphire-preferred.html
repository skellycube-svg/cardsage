When the CSP is worth keeping

Keep it if you spend $2,000+/year on combined dining and travel. The 3x multiplier on both categories is generous — most cards cap 3x to one or two categories. Sapphire Preferred gives you both unlimited.

The card is especially valuable if you're a CSR downgrader. You keep the same transfer partners (Hyatt, United, Southwest) so your points don't lose value. You just lose lounge access and the $300 travel credit. For people who don't use those, the Preferred is a no-brainer.

The $50 annual hotel credit via Chase Travel portal is the cherry on top. It's not massive, but it covers about half the fee on its own.

When you should upgrade or downgrade

If you travel 4+ times per year and spend $1,500+/month on the card, upgrade to the Sapphire Reserve ($795/yr). The lounge access and travel credit change the math significantly.

If you spend less than $2,000/year on dining and travel combined, consider the Chase Freedom Unlimited ($0 fee) instead. You lose transfer partners but also lose the fee.

Protection that matters

  • Trip delay insurance — up to $500 after 12-hour flight delay for meals and lodging.
  • Trip cancellation insurance — up to $10,000 per person for non-refundable travel.
  • Purchase protection — items damaged or stolen within 120 days, up to $500/claim.
  • Primary car rental insurance — primary CDW up to $60,000 outside the US.
  • Extended warranty — adds 1 extra year to manufacturer warranties of 3 years or less.

These protections are valuable for frequent travelers. Trip cancellation insurance alone could save you thousands.

The bottom line

The Sapphire Preferred is one of the best value transfer cards available. If you eat out and travel regularly, it pays for itself quickly through points alone. The credits and protections are nice bonuses. Highly recommended for people who don't need lounge access or heavy travel credits.
